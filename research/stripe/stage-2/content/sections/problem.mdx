The best record of the problem is the launch coverage, because it was written before anyone knew how the story ended. On 30 September 2011 TechCrunch reported a new payments company backed by $2 million from Peter Thiel, Elon Musk, Sequoia and others, and described what it removed: "you don't need a merchant account or gateway and Stripe will handle everything" <Evidence id="pricing.launch.2011" />.

That sentence is the problem statement. To take a card payment on a website in 2011, you needed a merchant account, which meant a bank, an application, and time. You needed a gateway to pass card numbers to that bank. And the card numbers passed through your own servers, which made you responsible for keeping them safe. None of that had anything to do with the thing you were building.

## The wedge

Stripe's answer was a price and a promise. The price was 2.9% plus 30 cents per successful charge, with "no setup fees, no monthly fees, no card storage fees, and no hidden costs" <Evidence id="pricing.launch.2011" />. The promise was that card details went straight to Stripe and never touched the developer's server, and that money arrived in the bank on a rolling seven day cycle.

The founders' own framing is worth quoting, because it names who they were selling to: "We believe that enabling transactions on the web is a problem rooted in code, not finance" <Evidence id="pricing.launch.2011" />. The buyer was the person writing the checkout, not the person who signed the bank forms.

## Why then

<Inference>
The launch article tells you what Stripe did and what it charged. It doesn't tell you why 2011 was the moment. My reading is that the buyer had changed: by then, the person who decided how a web business took money was increasingly the developer building it, and the incumbents still sold through banks to finance departments. Stripe sold to the coder. That's consistent with everything in the coverage, but it's an inference from one contemporaneous source, not something anyone measured.
</Inference>

## What hasn't changed

Fifteen years later the US pricing page says 2.9% + 30¢ per successful transaction, and that Stripe "does not charge setup fees, monthly fees, or any other hidden fees" <Evidence id="pricing.us.2026" />. The problem Stripe was founded on is still the problem it prices against. What's changed is what it sells on top, which is the rest of this teardown.
