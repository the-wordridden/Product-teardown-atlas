Stripe's loop is the mirror image of Figma's. For Figma, one link was proven and the rest were missing. For Stripe, every link is documented, by Stripe, in its own product documentation, and not one has a rate from anyone else. So the chain is drawn with four dashed links and one gap, and the honest headline is: **the machine is visible, the numbers on it are all Stripe's.**

## Around the ring

**Signing up to a first charge: partly proven.** There's no contract and no fixed fee, so the path from signup to a first live payment is just the integration <Evidence id="pricing.us.2026" />. Stripe now seeds it from AI coding tools: more than 100,000 claimable sandboxes have been created from tools like Replit and Vercel, each convertible into a live account <Evidence id="sandboxes.2025" />. And 20% of Atlas companies charged a first customer within 30 days, up from 8% in 2020 <Evidence id="atlas.2025" />. The mechanism is documented and frictionless by design. Every rate on it is Stripe's, measured on a cohort Stripe chose. Sandboxes created is not accounts activated.

**A first charge to ongoing processing: not established.** Nothing in the record measures how many activated businesses keep processing, reach meaningful volume, or how long it takes. Stripe's cohort claims describe revenue growth of businesses already at scale, which is a different thing. The only independent evidence about what happens after early charges is practitioners describing holds and closures <Evidence id="hn.account-holds" />, which tells you about the failure mode and nothing about the rate. This is the gap in the ring.

**Processing to Link and Radar: partly proven.** Each payment through Link enrols the consumer with Stripe, so they're recognised at the next business on Stripe <Evidence id="docs.link.enrolment" />. Stripe reports more than 200 million enrolled <Evidence id="link.users.feb-2026" /> and one merchant, Lovable, with 58% of its volume arriving through Link <Evidence id="lovable.link-share.2026" />. The Radar half of this link is Stripe's claim that "each payment makes the next payment safer" and that there's a better than 92% chance a card has been seen on the network before <Evidence id="radar.network.claim.2024" />. I count the Link mechanism, which is documented; I don't count the Radar claim, which isn't measured by anyone else.

**Data to attached products: partly proven.** Capital is prequalified every day from the account's own processing history, three months minimum, and repaid from future charges <Evidence id="docs.capital.underwriting" />. Billing, Tax and Radar are priced as extra percentages on volume already flowing <Evidence id="pricing.products.2026" />. Stripe says the Revenue suite passed $500 million and is heading to $1 billion <Evidence id="revenue-suite.run-rate.2024" /> <Evidence id="revenue-suite.run-rate.2026" />. The attach mechanism and its pricing are verified. Attach rates aren't disclosed, and the $1 billion is a forecast.

**Platforms and Link to the next business: partly proven, weakly.** A Standard connected account on a platform like Shopify is a full Stripe account with its own Dashboard <Evidence id="docs.connect.standard-accounts" />, so a platform's seller becomes a direct Stripe customer. A Link-enrolled consumer arrives at a new merchant already recognised. And Stripe says businesses on Stripe pay each other 4.8 million times a day <Evidence id="b2b-payments.daily.2026" />. Both mechanisms are product structure. No source says how many accounts came in through a platform, or that Link recognition makes a merchant choose Stripe. The step from structure to acquisition is the one nobody has measured.

## What the open ring means

Stripe's loop closes on paper. The product is built so that each payment leaves something behind, and each thing left behind is priced. What I can't do is put a rate on any transition without taking Stripe's word for it, and the one stage where independent evidence exists, the stretch after a first charge, is where the stories are about accounts being held.

Cycle time is unestablished. Nothing measures it.

<Judgment>
The most interesting thing in this loop isn't a link, it's a node: Link. Two hundred million consumers enrolled with Stripe rather than with any merchant is an asset that grows with every checkout, compounds across businesses, and, as the moats section shows, doesn't leave when a merchant does. If Stripe has a growth loop that works the way the letters imply, that's the part turning it. I'd want one number, any number, from outside Stripe before I called it proven.
</Judgment>
