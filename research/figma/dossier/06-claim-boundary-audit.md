### Premise correction

This audit was requested on the basis that a "Stage 1C" pass had produced strong mechanism-level evidence for problem, users, product behaviour, collaboration utility and switching costs. **No Stage 1C occurred.** The dossier contains three files. Research Categories C (archive), E (independent journalism) and F (community) have never been swept, and no direct product observation has been performed.

This audit is therefore conducted against the evidence that exists. Most rows resolve to INSUFFICIENT. That is the accurate result, and recording it is the point of the exercise.

### Adopted vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **EVIDENCED** | Supported by an opened primary or corroborated source at the claim's own altitude |
| **PARTIALLY EVIDENCED** | Some component supported; the claim as stated exceeds the evidence |
| **CONTESTED** | Credible evidence points in more than one direction |
| **INSUFFICIENT** | No evidence bearing on the claim. **Not the same as false** |

## 1. Language audit

The three dossier files were checked for causal and evaluative language exceeding the evidence: `proves`, `disproved`, `dominant`, `drives`, `causes`, `the reason Figma succeeded`, `high moat`, `strong moat`. **No instances found.** Existing phrasing already reads "discriminates between none of them," "position is not a mechanism," and "§6.4 cannot be run."

The four-term vocabulary above is adopted going forward, replacing the STRONG/MODERATE/WEAK scale used in `05` for claim-level classification. The section-level ceiling table in `05` retains its own scale, which measures *evidence availability by domain* rather than individual claim status — a different quantity.

## 2. Network effect classification — four-way split

| Class | Status | Precise wording |
|---|---|---|
| **Global network effect** | **INSUFFICIENT** | Current evidence does not support a global network-effect claim. No evidence suggests an existing user gains value when an unrelated external user joins. **This is untested, not disproved** |
| **Local / clustered network effect** | **INSUFFICIENT** | Evidence supports the existence of a multiplayer collaboration capability within a document [S001]. Whether intra-organisational collaboration utility satisfies the formal Atlas definition in §6.4 **remains a synthesis classification and cannot be resolved on current evidence** |
| **Cross-side network effect** | **INSUFFICIENT** | Separate Dev and Full seats evidence that Figma packages for designer and developer roles [S007]. Whether designer↔developer utility constitutes a cross-side network effect is **untested against the formal test** |
| **Workflow embedding / switching cost** | **INSUFFICIENT** | Treated separately from network effects throughout. See §5 |

**The decisive §6.4 question — does an existing user gain value when a *specific additional* user joins, and must that user be inside their own organisation — has no bearing evidence from any source gathered.** Answering it requires product observation and practitioner evidence, not filings.

## 3. Growth loop

**Status preserved: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.**

Canonical model retained unchanged: Acquisition → Activation → Core Action → Output → Reinvestment → Acquisition.

Three of five transitions have no supporting evidence. Output → Reinvestment is the only moderately supported link, resting on NDR of 136–139% and cohort growth [S007, S009, S005]. No non-canonical loop is substituted. Retention and expansion are **not** reinterpreted as acquisition. Ring closure is not forced.

**This is a valid analytical outcome and should survive into the teardown as a finding.** A public company that asserts "product virality inherent to Figma's collaborative and browser-based platform" [S007] while disclosing no acquisition mechanism, conversion data, or attribution is itself the observation.

## 4. Strategic causality

The statement submitted for audit does not appear in any dossier file. The standing bounded formulation, recorded here as the evidence-layer position:

> **The research provides evidence for product capability, a multiplayer collaboration architecture, and market position, but does not establish a self-reinforcing viral acquisition loop, any defensibility mechanism, or any causal account of Figma's growth.**

Any eventual statement about what drives Figma's success is **L5 evaluation → JUDGMENT by construction** (§4.2), belongs in synthesis, and must be marked as judgment.

## 6. Product observation audit

**No direct product observation has been performed.** Figma's product has never been opened in this research.

Every product-layer claim in the dossier derives from documentation: the 2019 engineering post [S001], the pricing page [S002], and the 10-K business section [S007]. All are correctly attributed as documentary.

**No observation-derived claim has been transformed into business, retention, acquisition, or monetisation impact** — because no observation-derived claims exist. This is a clean result and simultaneously a gap: Sections 5 (Product) and 4 (JTBD) require observation that has not occurred.

## 7. JTBD audit

**No candidate JTBDs exist to audit.** `05` records Section 4 as EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT with "no evidentiary basis whatsoever," and no job statements have been drafted.

Applied prospectively, the five-part test — user, circumstance, job, desired outcome, displaced alternative — is currently satisfiable on **none** of its components by any gathered source. Seat packaging [S007] weakly indicates perceived workflow boundaries, but packaging reflects monetisation strategy as much as user need.

**No JTBD may be retained on the grounds that it plausibly describes the product.**

## 9. Final research readiness

### READY FOR SYNTHESIS

Claims that can safely enter the synthesis layer now:

- **Vitals** — public status, ticker, structure, financials, geography
- **Business model** — per-seat subscription, six seat types, four plans, AI credits with enforcement date, full financials with periods and bases
- **The 2019 collaboration architecture**, with its documented limitations and its 2019 as-of date
- **Browser/multiplayer sequencing** — separable, sequential decisions
- **The AI margin trade-off** — first-party, explicit, with the cost arithmetic
- **The CMA market-boundary tension** (row 15) — the strongest analytical finding currently available
- **The growth loop's non-closure**, as a finding in its own right

### NEEDS ONE TARGETED QUESTION

Two, and only where the answer would materially change a conclusion:

1. **Does a free Viewer convert to a paid seat, and is there any evidence of the mechanism?** This single question determines whether a growth loop can be authored at all. Sources: practitioner accounts, archived pricing and role documentation, independent reporting.
2. **Is there evidence that customers experience material switching friction (components C and D)?** This determines whether switching costs can be claimed as a mechanism rather than remaining a plausible hypothesis. Sources: practitioner and migration accounts, review corpora.

### INSUFFICIENT

Genuinely unanswerable on available evidence, and not worth a research programme:

- **Management intent** behind the browser, multiplayer, IPO and dual-class decisions. Pre-IPO rationale was never disclosed; the S-1 might help marginally but is blocked [FIG-X007]
- **Segment composition and seat mix** — not disclosed and unlikely to become so
- **Formal network-effect classification** — requires evidence about value accrual between specific users that no available source produces
- **Any causal account of Figma's growth** — L4 causation, capped at INFERENCE by construction (§6.1), and currently without even inference-grade support
