This is the section this teardown exists for, and it produced the cleanest result in the Atlas so far: four links documented, one of them properly proven, and one missing in a way that matters.

## Around the chain

**Search to the template page: partly proven.** The gallery has a landing page per intent, 348 categories deep, holding over 30,000 templates <Evidence id="gallery.scale.2026" />. A third-party analysis using Ahrefs estimates the section at over 287,000 monthly visits, ranking for more than 60,000 US keywords, with 823,000 backlinks <Evidence id="gallery.traffic.ahrefs.2023" />. The demand is real: 100,000 global monthly searches for "notion templates", 7,000 for "notion templates for students" <Evidence id="gallery.keywords.ahrefs" />. These are tool estimates, not measurements, and I've marked them that way.

**The page to wanting it: partly proven.** What you land on isn't a brochure. A template page shows the full description, the creator, ratings and categories <Evidence id="gallery.template-page.observed" />, and links to the artefact itself on the creator's published site, where I watched headings, an hourly schedule and working databases render without ever signing in <Evidence id="gallery.artefact.observed" />. You get the whole thing before you get the wall.

**Wanting it to signing up: proven.** This is the one. Notion's own guide: if you're signed in, the template lands in your sidebar; "If you're logged out or don't have a Notion account, you'll be prompted to sign in or create an account first" <Evidence id="gallery.signup-gate" />. I clicked it and watched the button route to the account wall. Documented by the company, corroborated by observation. The template *is* the signup prompt.

**Signing up to using it: not established.** Nothing measures what happens after the duplication. No retention figure, no share who come back. The only independent evidence about this stage runs the other way: a team lead describing the flexibility as cognitive overhead and going back to Trello <Evidence id="flexibility.cost.2020" />, and a report of companies abandoning Notion over speed <Evidence id="block.sharding.2021" />. This is the gap in the chain.

**Using it to making one: partly proven.** Creator profiles went from about 2,000 at the 2023 relaunch to over 22,000 now <Evidence id="gallery.scale.2023" /> <Evidence id="gallery.scale.2026" />, and submission is a form with a public link. What share of users ever publish, nobody says.

**And back to search.** Every accepted template is another indexed page targeting another intent, which is how 600 templates became 30,000. Whether the inventory produced the traffic or the traffic attracted the inventory isn't established by either source.

## The number, and the number that's missing

Notion has disclosed exactly one figure about this machine, once, in 2023: **"51 million template duplications from nearly 11 million people"** in a single year <Evidence id="gallery.duplications.2023" />. The gallery held 5,000 templates then. It holds six times that now, and there has been no update.

Now put that next to the signup gate. Every one of those duplications by a logged-out person required creating an account. So the loop converts, by construction, at a scale of millions.

What Notion has never said is how many of those eleven million people were new.

<Judgment>
That omission is the finding. A company that publishes its CRDT operation rate per minute, and publishes duplications and unique duplicators, has the new-versus-existing split sitting in the same query. It chose the metric that sounds like acquisition without being it.

I'm not saying the gallery doesn't acquire users. I'd guess it does, heavily, and the search traffic makes that plausible. But a guess is what it would be. The honest version is: this is the best documented conversion surface I've examined and the least documented acquisition channel, and those two facts are about the same machine.
</Judgment>

Cycle time is unestablished. Nothing measures how long any step takes.
