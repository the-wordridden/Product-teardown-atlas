The best account of the problem isn't Notion's. It's a comment on Hacker News from January 2019, where a user described what the product sat between: "the perfect balance between strict JIRA/PM tools and completely unstructured wikis/text files" <Evidence id="positioning.between-tools.2019" />.

That sentence names a real gap. On one side, trackers that make you adopt someone else's process. On the other, documents that hold anything and track nothing. Teams ran both and maintained the seam by hand.

## What people were actually leaving

The same thread is full of it, first-hand and dated. A team that "switched to it after getting annoyed with Asana and not wanting to use something like Jira" <Evidence id="jtbd.team-tasks.2019" />. Another using it "in lieu of JIRA." On the personal side: "For me, Notion replaced Evernote and I haven't looked back", and a user who "used to draft emails in an unorganized OneNote notebook" and had to search and hope he remembered the right keyword <Evidence id="jtbd.personal-wiki.2019" />.

Asana, Jira, Evernote, OneNote, Google Docs, Dropbox Paper. Six tools in one thread, all being replaced by one thing.

## The wedge

One primitive. A block is any piece of content, blocks nest inside blocks, and a page is itself a block <Evidence id="block.api.primitive" />. That's the whole idea: if a paragraph, a task, a table row and a page are the same kind of object, then a document and a database stop being different products.

A user in that thread described it better than the marketing did: everything is a block, and blocks nest and attach to make pages, tables, kanban boards and calendars <Evidence id="positioning.between-tools.2019" />.

## Why then

My reading is that the gap had been widening for years as teams accumulated one tool per job, and someone finally sold the seam instead of another tool. That's consistent with the accounts, but nobody measured it, and Notion's own framing is a mission statement about early computing pioneers rather than a dated problem statement <Evidence id="notion.no-numbers" />.
