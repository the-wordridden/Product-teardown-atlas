## The seat machine

Figma sells access by the seat, monthly or annually, across four plans and five seat types. The View seat is the only free one: you can look and comment, not edit <Evidence id="seat.view.free" />. Paid seats map to roles. Collab for whiteboarding and presenting, Dev for inspecting and handing off, Full for everything. List prices in August 2026 ran from $3 to $90 a seat a month depending on plan and type <Evidence id="pricing.list.2026" />. List price only. It says nothing about what a large customer actually negotiates.

The moment money changes hands is precise, documented, and unusually frictionless: **the step from free viewing to a paid seat**. Grant someone edit access and they get a billed seat. If that seat type isn't available, Figma adds it to the subscription. And approval can be set to fully automatic <Evidence id="seat.approval-settings" />. Practitioners in two separate forum threads, four years apart, describe living through exactly that. Upgrades happening without the person paying noticing until the invoice lands <Evidence id="seat.upgrade-billing.practitioner" />. That's Tier 5 evidence and I've admitted it with the limitation stated. It backs up the documentation rather than contradicting it.

Since March 2026 there's a usage layer on top: AI credits, metered per seat, with paid top ups. The seat is still the unit you buy. The model just isn't purely per seat anymore.

## The economics, as disclosed

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $1,055.8 million, up 41% <Evidence id="revenue.fy2025" /> <Evidence id="revenue.growth.fy2025" />. Expansion looks strong on its own terms. Net dollar retention was 136% in June 2026 <Evidence id="ndr.q2-2026" />, and the group spending over $100,000 grew 46% year on year <Evidence id="paid-customers.100k.q2-2026" />.

The margin line is the part that repays a second look. Cost of revenue grew 112% while revenue grew 41% <Evidence id="cost-of-revenue.fy2025" />, and $49.1 million of that increase was AI infrastructure and hosting. Gross margin, worked out from the audited figures, fell from 88.3% in fiscal 2024 <Evidence id="gross-margin.fy2024" /> to 82.4% in fiscal 2025 <Evidence id="gross-margin.fy2025" />. And Figma says this isn't a blip. The 10-K states the company **expects** AI costs to push gross and operating margins down <Evidence id="ai-margin-warning.fy2025" />. A forward looking admission against its own interest, which is worth more than any analyst's estimate.

## What these numbers don't prove

Net dollar retention of 136% is a financial metric. It fits with switching costs, with network effects, with workflow habit, and with people simply liking the product. It can't tell those apart. It isn't evidence of viral growth, product market fit, or a moat. It's evidence that accounts already paying Figma pay it more over time. Likewise, the seat mechanism above is an evidenced way of growing inside existing accounts. It's not a growth model for the whole company, and the Growth Loops section shows exactly where the rest of that model is missing.

The shape I see in the disclosed numbers: a seat led expansion business that's knowingly trading gross margin for an AI position, and saying so in print. Where the margin stops falling isn't established.
