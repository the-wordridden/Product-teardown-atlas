Two jobs, both from teams that switched to Figma and explained why in their own words. That's a small sample, so treat these as well grounded examples rather than a census.

## Job one: one live file for a spread out team

*When my design team is spread across offices, help us all work in one live file so we stop juggling versions and handoff tools.*

This is Intercom's story almost word for word <Evidence id="migration.intercom.2019" />. Nineteen designers in three cities, on Sketch, wrapped in a tool ecosystem the team called "prohibitively messy and limiting." Files named final-final-ver.99, hidden in twenty folders. What pulled them was the flat document system, no files to save, open from any browser.

What they were nervous about is just as telling. Losing their Sketch plugins. An interface they found unpolished. One to two months of adjustment pain, which they went through anyway. The alternative they left was Sketch plus separate tools for storage and handoff.

## Job two: a design system that bends without breaking

*When I maintain a design system, let components be adjusted per instance so I stop building workarounds for rigid overrides.*

Help Scout's principal designer ran their migration and wrote it up <Evidence id="migration.helpscout.2019" />. The specific reason: Sketch only allowed pre set overrides, so their system had grown complex nested components purely to work around that. In Figma, colour, type and effects can be changed on each instance while staying linked to the main component <Evidence id="docs.components" />.

The cost was real. Nested components didn't transfer. They retraced them from screenshots. About a week's work, to arrive at a simpler system.

<Inference>
Notice what both jobs have in common. Neither is "design something beautiful." Both are about a team's plumbing: versions, handoffs, overrides, libraries. Two accounts isn't proof, but it's a consistent hint that Figma gets hired to fix how a team works together before it gets hired for what it can draw.
</Inference>

Both accounts are from 2019 and from the design side of the team. What developers or product managers hire Figma for isn't covered by anything I found first hand.
