Neither job is "design something beautiful." Both are about a team's plumbing. Two teams that switched and explained why in their own words, from 2019 and from the design side.

## Job one: one live file for a spread out team

*When my design team is spread across offices, help us all work in one live file so we stop juggling versions and handoff tools.*

Intercom: 19 designers in three cities on Sketch, in a tool ecosystem they called "prohibitively messy and limiting", with files named final-final-ver.99 <Evidence id="migration.intercom.2019" />. The pull was no files to save and any browser to open. The anxiety: losing Sketch plugins, an unpolished interface, one to two months of adjustment. They went through it anyway.

## Job two: a design system that bends without breaking

*When I maintain a design system, let components be adjusted per instance so I stop building workarounds for rigid overrides.*

Sketch allowed only preset overrides, so Help Scout had built complex nested components to work around it <Evidence id="migration.helpscout.2019" />. In Figma, colour, type and effects change per instance while staying linked <Evidence id="docs.components" />. The cost: nested components didn't transfer, and they retraced them from screenshots. About a week, to arrive at a simpler system.

<Inference>
Figma gets hired to fix how a team works together before it gets hired for what it can draw. Two accounts isn't proof, but it's a consistent hint.
</Inference>
