The moat stack for this teardown is empty, and the exact wording matters: **no moat mechanism has been established from the available evidence.** That's not the same as "Figma has no moat," which would be a stronger claim than I can make in the other direction.

## Why the stack is empty

To count as a moat here, a claim needs four things: a named mechanism, evidence it actually operates, an argument for why it's hard to copy, and a named way to attack it. Four candidates clear the first bar. None clears the rest.

**Collaboration capability.** A verified capability with unresolved defensibility. The multiplayer architecture is documented in unusual detail, by Figma itself <Evidence id="architecture.multiplayer.2019" />. That cuts both ways. The capability is real, and so is the public roadmap for building it again.

**Technical architecture.** Also a verified capability, moat status unresolved. Browser native rendering from 2015, custom centralised sync by 2019. Serious engineering, publicly described, and seven years old as evidence.

**Ecosystem.** A verified capability, defensibility unresolved. There's a large public plugin and template catalogue, which I saw myself <Evidence id="community.public-surface" />. But the integration surface is built largely on open protocols that any competitor can use. The openness that grows the ecosystem also lowers the cost of building a rival one.

**Market position.** Evidenced, and not a mechanism. The strongest defensibility adjacent evidence is the UK competition authority's 2023 finding that Figma was "the clear market leader… several times larger than any other supplier" of all in one screen design software <Evidence id="cma.market-position.2023" />. Position tells you where a company stands. A moat tells you why it can't be moved. The CMA finding gives you the first.

## Four things I won't blur together

**Collaboration isn't a network effect.** The formal test asks whether an existing user gets more value when a specific new user joins. The architecture shows two people can edit one file. Nothing I gathered shows the first person's value going up because the second one arrived. The test fails at that step, so I'm not classifying a network effect in either direction.

**A feature existing isn't a switching cost.** Design system management is clearly there as an Enterprise capability <Evidence id="product-surface.10k" />. But a switching cost claim needs four parts: the asset exists, moving it takes effort, customers actually feel the friction, and the friction has a cost. Only the first is established. The last has no supporting evidence across any of nine candidate mechanisms I checked. One hands on migration write up shows partial asset transfer. I found no account of a whole organisation trying to leave.

**Retention isn't defensibility.** Net dollar retention of 136% <Evidence id="ndr.q2-2026" /> fits switching costs, network effects, habit, and plain satisfaction equally. It can't tell you which.

## The counter evidence

Figma's own risk factors don't let you rest easy. The 10-K names "design-to-code and AI-driven companies," point tools that "can expand to cover more," and customers' own internal tools as competitive threats. It admits pricing power "may be constrained by competitive dynamics" <Evidence id="ai-margin-warning.fy2025" />. Notice that several of those threats fall in categories the CMA's 2023 market definition explicitly left out. So the independent evidence of market leadership was produced against a boundary the company's own disclosures no longer seem to accept.

<EvidenceGap>
**What we can establish:** four capabilities. A 2023 finding of market leadership. Strong retention.

**What we cannot establish:** any mechanism that would stop a well funded competitor.

**Why the evidence is thin:** defensibility shows up in what customers do under pressure. Migrations attempted, abandoned, priced. I found no source of that kind.

**What would settle it:** organisation level migration stories, churn or win/loss data, or switching friction accounts with a cost attached.
</EvidenceGap>
