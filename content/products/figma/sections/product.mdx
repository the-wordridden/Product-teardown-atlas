## The objects

Everything in Figma lives in a **file**, and inside a file the basic unit is a **frame**: a container for shapes, text and images, nested as deep as you like. Frames aren't just boxes. Auto layout, constraints and prototyping all work on frames, so a frame is the thing you actually design with <Evidence id="docs.frames" />.

A **component** is a frame you've promised to reuse. The main component defines it; every **instance** is a linked copy that picks up changes to the main <Evidence id="docs.components" />. Publish components to a **team library** and every file in the team can use them. That's the whole design system story in one sentence, and it's why the migration accounts keep coming back to libraries.

## The actions

Edit a file with other people in it. Comment on a frame. Publish a library. Inspect a design in Dev Mode. Share a link. The first and last are the ones that matter most, and they're the same action seen from two sides: a link opens a live file, and anyone with the link is in it.

The moment it clicks, going by Intercom's account, is opening a colleague's file by link and realising there's nothing to download, version or send. They called it "the absence of files" <Evidence id="migration.intercom.2019" />.

## Under the hood, as of 2019

In October 2019, co-founder Evan Wallace explained how the sync engine works <Evidence id="architecture.multiplayer.2019" />. It's precise where most coverage is vague. Figma turned down operational transforms as "unnecessarily complex for our problem space." And it does **not** use true CRDTs, whatever you've read. Because the server is the single authority, Figma runs a simpler custom system that borrows CRDT ideas: one server process per document, WebSocket sync, client generated object IDs, fractional indexing for tree order, offline edits replayed on reconnect.

He also listed what it can't do, which is why this post is worth more than a marketing page:

- two people can't edit the same text value at once
- conflicting property changes resolve last write wins, no merging
- moving objects between parents at the same moment can make them vanish briefly ("not great," in his words)

Seven years on, nothing I found describes the current architecture. Whether those limits still hold is an open question with competitive weight.

What you can touch depends on your seat, from free View up through Collab, Dev and Full <Evidence id="seat.view.free" />. And there's a public Community catalogue of files, plugins and templates that anyone can browse without an account. I checked <Evidence id="community.public-surface" />.

Three sentences that sound alike and aren't: "Figma can sync several editors in one file" is a capability, and it's shown. "So users find live collaboration valuable" is about behaviour, and two teams say so. "Multiplayer is why Figma grew" is causal, and nothing here establishes it.

Time to value is the one thing in this section I've left unestablished. Intercom talks about one to two months to settle in, but that's adjustment, not first value, and nobody I read timed the first moment it paid off.
