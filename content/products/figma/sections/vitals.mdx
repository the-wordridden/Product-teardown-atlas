Figma is a San Francisco company <Evidence id="hq.10k" />, founded in 2012, and listed on the New York Stock Exchange as FIG since 2025. It sits in design collaboration. That placement isn't just mine. The UK competition authority looked at the company under the market definition of "all-in-one screen design software" <Evidence id="cma.market-position.2023" />.

Three numbers tell you the scale. Revenue for fiscal 2025 came in at $1,055.8 million <Evidence id="revenue.fy2025" />, up 41% on the year before <Evidence id="revenue.growth.fy2025" />. By mid 2026, 15,964 paid customers were spending more than $10,000 a year <Evidence id="paid-customers.10k.q2-2026" />, and that group was spending 36% more than it did twelve months earlier <Evidence id="ndr.q2-2026" />.

One thing to keep in mind whenever you see a Figma customer count. Figma counts each separately billed division or subsidiary as its own customer. So its numbers don't line up with other companies' numbers unless you check how they count too.

The company is public, but control isn't spread out. Class B shares carry fifteen votes each and Class C carry none. As of 31 December 2025, co-founder Dylan Field held about 72.3% of the voting power, including 24.4% he votes on his co-founder's behalf under an irrevocable proxy <Evidence id="voting-power.field" />. That's a snapshot. The filing says it may grow as equity awards settle.

Here's the thing I'd flag before you read any other number: fiscal 2025's GAAP figures aren't a clean read on how the business runs. They carry a one time $975.7 million stock compensation charge from the IPO, so any year on year comparison built on net loss looks worse than it is. When I talk about margins in the business model section, I use gross margin arithmetic instead. The distortion there is smaller and the trend is real.
