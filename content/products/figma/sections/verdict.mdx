## What the evidence suggests

<Inference>
The numbers fit an expansion led growth model better than an acquisition led one. Retention is measured only on the 2% of customers above $10,000 a year. The one evidenced mechanism works inside paying accounts. And the acquisition side of the loop is unestablished after a specific search. That's an inference from the pattern of what is and isn't disclosed. Not a proven fact about how Figma actually grows.
</Inference>

## What stays open

Any mechanism for winning new customers. Any job to be done. Any moat. Any network effect classification, either way. Who the users are. What the current architecture looks like. Whether switching costs actually bite. And why any pre IPO decision was made.

## My judgment

<Judgment>
The central call in this teardown, mine and not a fact: **Figma's one evidenced growth engine is seat expansion inside existing accounts, while the product virality its own filings claim is, after a dedicated research pass, still just a company claim.**

Two sentences need to stay apart. "Figma's filings claim product virality" is a verified fact <Evidence id="gtm.two-motions" />. "A viral acquisition mechanism exists" is a separate proposition this research couldn't establish. Figma may well have one. Absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence, and the missing evidence (behaviour and attribution data) is exactly the kind companies rarely publish. But as things stand, the loudest claim in Figma's growth story is the only part resting entirely on Figma's word.

What I'd most want answered next, in the order it would change my mind: whether free viewers turn into *new accounts* and not just new seats; whether any organisation has tried to leave and what it cost them; and whether the 2019 architecture's limits still hold. The first would close or break the loop. The second would put something in the moat stack, or confirm it's empty. The third would date stamp the product's strongest claim to being technically different.

A note on how this verdict was reached. It says less than most published Figma analysis, on purpose. Every stronger version I drafted needed at least one claim the evidence couldn't carry.
</Judgment>

The five that shape this verdict most: the acquisition mechanism, switching cost consequences, current architecture, who the users are, and what Figma does with the 98% of paying customers below the measured line.
