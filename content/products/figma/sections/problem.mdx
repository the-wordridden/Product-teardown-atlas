**Figma shipped a browser tool first and collaboration second.** Any story where it launched as a collaboration product squashes two decisions into one.

At its December 2015 preview, Figma said software should be "online, real-time, and collaborative", and described design tools as lagging the workflows engineers already had. But it announced multiplayer editing as arriving in 2016, not something you could use at launch <Evidence id="multiplayer.announced.2016" />.

The trigger was technical. The founders' moment was an April 2011 demo of GPU-accelerated image processing in a browser through WebGL: a graphics demo, not a collaboration prototype. Browser creative tools had been stuck on rendering performance, not demand, and WebGL unstuck them <Evidence id="architecture.multiplayer.2019" />.

<Judgment>
The sequencing is a fact: browser access, then multiplayer. Whether "access rather than collaboration" was the original strategic wedge is not established, and any reading of why it was sequenced that way would go past the evidence. The account of the world before Figma (single-player desktop tools, work split across apps) is the company's own.
</Judgment>
