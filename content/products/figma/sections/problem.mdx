Every account of the problem Figma set out to solve comes from Figma. That doesn't make it wrong. It does make it a company story, and I've treated it as one.

## What we can actually pin down

In December 2015, at its preview release, Figma said software should be "online, real-time, and collaborative." It described design tools as lagging behind the collaborative workflows engineers already had. And it announced simultaneous multiplayer editing as something arriving in 2016, not something you could use at launch <Evidence id="multiplayer.announced.2016" />.

That last detail is the most useful fact in this section. The browser decision and the multiplayer decision happened one after the other. Figma shipped a design tool that ran in a browser first. Real time collaboration came later. Any version of the story where Figma launched as a collaboration product is squashing two decisions into one.

The technical trigger is dated too. The founders' moment was an April 2011 demo of GPU accelerated image processing running in a browser through WebGL. A graphics demo, not a collaboration prototype. Browser based creative tools had been stuck on rendering performance, not on whether anyone wanted them, and WebGL unstuck them <Evidence id="architecture.multiplayer.2019" />.

## What stays the company's word

<EvidenceGap>
**What we can establish:** Figma's own 2015 description of the world before it: single player desktop tools, and a workflow split across separate apps for creating, commenting, sharing and storing.

**What we cannot establish:** whether that description was fair. I didn't find an independent source from the time describing how designers actually worked between 2012 and 2015.

**Why the evidence is thin:** the strong sources here are filings, documentation and regulatory records. All of them either come after the founding era or come from Figma.

**What would settle it:** practitioner accounts from the period, archived documentation for the tools people used then, or independent workflow research.
</EvidenceGap>

<Judgment>
The evidence establishes that Figma pursued browser native access and subsequently introduced multiplayer collaboration as separate, sequential product developments. It does not establish that "access rather than collaboration" was the original strategic wedge. The sequencing is a fact. Any reading of why it was sequenced that way would go past the evidence, so I'm not offering one.
</Judgment>
