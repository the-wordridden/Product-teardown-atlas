Most teardowns draw a closed loop because a closed loop looks confident. This one is drawn open, because that's what the evidence supports. The gap in the ring isn't a hole in the analysis. It's the result.

The question here isn't "what is Figma's growth loop?" It's "can the loop actually be evidenced?" After a research pass aimed squarely at that: **one transition out of five.**

## Around the ring

**Getting to Acquisition: insufficient.** Figma's 10-K names two ways in: self serve through Figma.com, and a direct sales team <Evidence id="gtm.two-motions" />. There's also a public Community page anyone can reach without an account, with a "Remix" button on it. I looked <Evidence id="community.public-surface" />. But a door isn't a mechanism. There's no channel data, no conversion path, no case study showing how a non user becomes a user. The same filing says Figma has "product virality inherent to Figma's collaborative and browser-based platform." I went looking for that mechanism specifically. It's a claim without anything behind it that I could find.

**Acquisition to Activation: insufficient.** Nothing defines what activation is. And the behaviour that would show the proposed mechanism, opening a privately shared file without an account, is something I couldn't observe.

**Activation to Core Action: partially evidenced.** A free way to take part clearly exists. The View seat lets you look and comment at no cost <Evidence id="seat.view.free" />. Whether people move from there toward paid editing isn't evidenced.

**Core Action to Output: insufficient.** That design work produces things that get passed to non users is an inference from how the product is shaped. Nothing shows it happening, or how often.

**Output to Reinvestment: evidenced.** This is the one link that holds, and it holds well. Free viewers inside a paying account become billed seats through a documented path that can be set to approve itself <Evidence id="seat.approval-settings" />. Practitioners confirm it across 2021 to 2025 <Evidence id="seat.upgrade-billing.practitioner" />. And you can see it in the numbers: 136% net dollar retention <Evidence id="ndr.q2-2026" /> and 34% growth in the over $10,000 group <Evidence id="paid-customers.10k.q2-2026" />.

**Reinvestment to Acquisition: insufficient.** Revenue visibly funds R&D and AI infrastructure <Evidence id="rnd.fy2025" />. Whether that spending brings in new customers isn't shown. A company spending on R&D isn't proof that R&D acquires anyone.

## What the open ring means

The one evidenced transition happens **inside** an account that already pays. Every practitioner story involves an admin, an invoice, and a subscription that was already there. Growing seats isn't the same as winning customers, and treating it as the same would close this ring under false pretences. That's the move this section won't make.

What the evidence does support is an **expansion engine**: strong, documented, low friction, and fenced in by the walls of accounts that already pay. How those accounts came to exist is, right now, unestablished. So is how fast the loop turns. Nothing measures how long a viewer takes to become a billed editor.

<Judgment>
The gap between Figma's stated growth story and its evidenced growth mechanism is the single most interesting thing this research turned up. A public filing claims virality. A focused search found an expansion mechanism and no acquisition mechanism. That doesn't prove the virality claim is false. Absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence. But it does mean the claim currently rests on Figma's word alone, and I'd weight it that way.
</Judgment>
