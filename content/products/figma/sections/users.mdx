Three kinds of people pay for Figma, and a fourth kind uses it for free. You can read that straight off how it's sold.

## Who shows up

**Product design teams** are the core. Two teams that switched and wrote it up look the same: distributed designers keeping a shared component library and working in one live file. Intercom had 19 designers across Dublin, London and San Francisco <Evidence id="migration.intercom.2019" />. Help Scout was fully remote with a design system built as nested libraries <Evidence id="migration.helpscout.2019" />. Full seats, the ones that unlock the design editor, are built for them <Evidence id="seat.view.free" />.

**Developers** get their own seat. Dev Mode is sold separately, which is Figma telling you engineers are a distinct paying group, not a side effect of designers <Evidence id="product-surface.10k" />.

**Reviewers and stakeholders** sit on free View seats. Product managers, marketers, clients: anyone who looks and comments but never edits <Evidence id="seat.view.free" />. They're documented. They're not counted.

The power user, going by both accounts, is a team that has stopped thinking about files. They publish a library, work across time zones in the same document, and hand off through inspect instead of exports. Intercom's phrase for the shift was "the absence of files."

Who it isn't for: a designer working alone and offline. That's my inference from how the thing is built (a live browser file, with offline as a reconnect mode) rather than something anyone said.

## The numbers

About 690,000 paid customers as of March 2026 <Evidence id="paid-customers.total.q1-2026" />. Of those, 15,964 spent over $10,000 a year by June 2026 <Evidence id="paid-customers.10k.q2-2026" />, and 1,635 spent over $100,000 <Evidence id="paid-customers.100k.q2-2026" />. Divide and you get the number worth remembering: **about 2% of paying customers sit above the $10,000 line, and the headline retention figure is calculated on that 2% alone** <Evidence id="ndr.q2-2026" />.

<Inference>
That means the Figma that gets measured is a thin slice of the whole. What the other 98% are worth to Figma is genuinely open. They could be a pipeline into the measured group, or a long tail that pays a little and stays. The data fits both.
</Inference>

One caveat on the shape above. The three segments come from seat packaging and two teams' accounts, so their relative sizes are my reading, not a disclosed mix. Figma doesn't publish seat counts by type.
