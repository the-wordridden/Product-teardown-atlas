One bet, not the four a Figma retrospective usually lists. A bet needs evidence for the whole chain: the choice, what it was meant to win, what it cost, what happened. For most of Figma's history I can show the decision but not the intent.

## Fund AI hard, meter it with credits: unresolved

**The bet.** Serious money into AI across the platform, the Weavy acquisition, AI credits on every seat in 2025, and enforced limits with paid top-ups from March 2026. Figma calls AI "already a tailwind" and "TAM-expansive": a stated hope, not an outcome.

**The cost, disclosed not discovered.** The 10-K says inference costs will hit cost of revenue, R&D and sales, and that Figma **expects margins to fall** <Evidence id="ai-margin-warning.fy2025" />. Figma priced its own bet in public.

**What's happened.** Cost of revenue up 112% against 41% revenue growth, $49.1 million of it AI infrastructure <Evidence id="cost-of-revenue.fy2025" />. Gross margin from 88.3% <Evidence id="gross-margin.fy2024" /> to 82.4% <Evidence id="gross-margin.fy2025" />. Not all of that is this bet, but the direction matches what Figma said.

**The tension.** The same filing names AI design-to-code tools as competitors. The technology Figma is funding is the technology most likely to squeeze it.

## What didn't make it

**Browser-native (2015)** and **multiplayer (announced for 2016)** are evidenced as separate, sequential decisions <Evidence id="multiplayer.announced.2016" />, but nothing from the time says what either was expected to win. **Dual-class control**, 72.3% of the vote <Evidence id="voting-power.field" />, is documented; why it was chosen isn't.

<Judgment>
The only bet with a complete chain is the one made while Figma had to disclose things. The historically interesting bets are the least evidenced, not because they were less deliberate but because private companies never show their reasoning. A teardown that narrates 2012 to 2015 as confident strategy is writing fiction with good production values.
</Judgment>
