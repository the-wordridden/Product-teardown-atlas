Stripe describes its users by size, not by type. The 2025 letter runs from "all of the top AI companies" through "90% of the Dow Jones Industrial Average" and "80% of the Nasdaq 100" to the quarter of new Delaware corporations that start life in Stripe Atlas <Evidence id="enterprise-penetration.2025" /> <Evidence id="atlas.2025" />. Any use of any product counts toward those percentages. They tell you Stripe is everywhere; they don't tell you who pays it most.

So I've split the users by how they arrive and how they're billed, which the record does support.

## Developer-led software businesses

The 2011 audience, and still the one Stripe writes to. They sign up themselves, integrate through the API, and pay per charge. Stripe's 2025 cohort was, in its words, the fastest moving it had ever seen, with 57% of new businesses outside the US <Evidence id="cohort.international.2025" />. Two new front doors serve this group: sandboxes created from AI coding tools like Replit and Vercel, more than 100,000 of them, which turn into live accounts <Evidence id="sandboxes.2025" />, and Atlas incorporations, up 41% <Evidence id="atlas.2025" />. This is the largest group by count on Stripe's own figures. Its share of volume is not disclosed.

## Platforms and marketplaces

Businesses whose customers are themselves businesses. Shopify's store owners, Airbnb's hosts, DoorDash's restaurants. Stripe names all three in its Connect documentation and prices the segment separately: $2 per active connected account per month, 0.25% plus 25 cents per payout <Evidence id="pricing.connect.2026" />. The documentation also settles something that matters for anyone in this group: who carries the risk. On direct charges the seller does; on destination charges the platform does; on the older Express and Custom account types the platform is "responsible for disputes and fraud" <Evidence id="docs.connect.charge-types" />.

## Enterprises on negotiated terms

The Dow and Nasdaq names don't pay 2.9%. The pricing page offers custom packages "for businesses with large payments volume or unique business models" <Evidence id="pricing.products.2026" />. The audited UK accounts give a rare glimpse of the motion behind that: sales is the biggest function there, 128 of 288 staff <Evidence id="spuk.employees.fy2024" />. One subsidiary, but a sold motion at the top is what it shows.

## Who wrote down why they came

One team, that I found. Apify, a web scraping company, chose Braintree first "since it was the only major global provider supporting European companies at the time," added Stripe when "Stripe caught up and expanded its capabilities," and kept both for redundancy <Evidence id="apify.migration.2025" />. Coverage and a second rail. That's the selection story in the record.

Stripe tells the other kind of story itself. Lovable, an AI app builder, runs Payments, Billing, usage billing, Atlas and Link, sells into more than 150 countries, and takes 58% of its volume through Link <Evidence id="lovable.link-share.2026" />. That's Stripe describing a customer, so it's their framing, but it's the clearest picture of the power user: global from day one, several products on one account.

## Who it isn't for

Practitioners are louder about leaving than arriving, and the accounts cluster. A fashion brand describing $50,000 held for "at least 120 days" after a few unusually large orders, with no phone number to call <Evidence id="hn.account-holds" />. A dating app that had been on Stripe for two years, found its whole category added to the restricted list, and lost recurring subscriptions when the account closed <Evidence id="hn.data-refusal.2024" />.

<Inference>
Two patterns in those accounts: small merchants whose volume jumps suddenly, and businesses in categories Stripe restricts or later decides to restrict. These are lived experiences, not rates. The same threads carry the counter-view that every online processor produces the same stories, and that a business without an account manager has chosen to be on the self-serve path. I think both things are true at once.
</Inference>
