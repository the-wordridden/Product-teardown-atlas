The number that hasn't moved: 2.9% plus 30 cents in September 2011 <Evidence id="pricing.launch.2011" />, and 2.9% + 30¢ today <Evidence id="pricing.us.2026" />. Everything around it has.

## The surcharges moved

International cards cost 1.5% extra, currency conversion 1%, manually entered cards 0.5% <Evidence id="pricing.surcharges.2026" />. The international surcharge was 1.0% until June 2023, when Stripe also stopped refunding the $15 dispute fee on disputes the merchant wins, while stressing that "there's no change to our standard 2.9% + $0.30 pricing" <Evidence id="pricing.change.june-2023" />. European and UK businesses got their own increases that April <Evidence id="pricing.change.eu.april-2023" />. The page announcing the European change now redirects to the pricing page.

<Inference>
That's where Stripe's pricing power lives. The headline is the number everyone compares, so it stays fixed. The components around it are hard to compare across providers, so that's where increases go.
</Inference>

## The stack priced on top

Every product is another slice of the same flow: Billing 0.7% of billed volume, Invoicing 0.4%, Radar from 5 cents a screen, Atlas $500 once <Evidence id="pricing.products.2026" />; Connect $2 per active account and 0.25% + 25¢ per payout <Evidence id="pricing.connect.2026" />. The Revenue suite passed a $500 million run rate in 2024 <Evidence id="revenue-suite.run-rate.2024" /> and is "on track" for $1 billion in 2026 <Evidence id="revenue-suite.run-rate.2026" />, with Billing running nearly 200 million subscriptions <Evidence id="billing.scale.2024" />.

## What the reported numbers imply

$5.1 billion of 2024 revenue <Evidence id="revenue.2024.reported" /> on $1.4 trillion of volume <Evidence id="tpv.2024" /> is a yield of about 0.4%, far below 2.9%, as you'd expect once interchange passes through and enterprises negotiate. Treat it as arithmetic on two reported figures, not a fact. The cash is the part I'd anchor on: about $3.2 billion of free cash flow in 2025 <Evidence id="fcf.2025.reported" />, which paid for four acquisitions without a funding round.

<Judgment>
Stripe is a transaction business with a software business attached, and the attachment is where the growth is. Every product adds a percentage to money already flowing, each priced separately, so nothing in the headline moves. It's a good model. It also means a customer's real rate is much harder to see than the number on the pricing page.
</Judgment>
