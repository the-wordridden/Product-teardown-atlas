Four bets. One is finished, and finished in public. Three are running, and every result on them is Stripe's own account.

## The PayPal bid

The dates come from Axios's reconstruction, and they line up with TechCrunch and the Wall Street Journal's reporting <Evidence id="paypal.bid.timeline.2026" />. Bloomberg reported a possible deal on 24 February 2026; Semafor said there were no talks two days later. Stripe and Advent approached PayPal in early April and got no answer. On 14 July, Reuters reported a bid of $60.50 a share, about $53 billion, a 39% premium to PayPal's price before the first rumour. PayPal called it insufficient <Evidence id="paypal.bid.rejected.2026" />. Talks continued. On 13 August PayPal's shares closed above the bid. On 27 August Stripe and Advent walked away, and PayPal fell 12% the next morning.

Axios's sourced explanation is the interesting part: the two sides never agreed on why the price had gone up. Stripe thought its own interest had moved it. PayPal thought its turnaround had. Whoever was right, a higher bid would have been paying for excitement the bid itself created.

Stripe never announced any of this, so there's no stated rationale to test. The nearest thing is The Information's July headline, which framed Stripe's $3.2 billion of cash as "setting up acquisition hunt" <Evidence id="fcf.2025.reported" />. I've marked the bet mixed rather than backfired. It cost nothing disclosed, it revealed an appetite nobody had seen, and it revealed a price Stripe wouldn't cross. Both are now on the record, and both will be read against the next one.

## Stablecoins: buy the rail

Bridge closed in February 2025 <Evidence id="bridge.acquired.2025" />. Privy followed, then Financial Accounts in more than 100 countries, then in September 2025 Tempo, a blockchain "purpose-built for payments" built with Paradigm <Evidence id="tempo.2025" />. Stripe's reasoning, in its letter: agents will soon make most internet transactions, and today's blockchains, designed for trading, can't carry them. Its own cautionary tale is a memecoin frenzy that delayed one Bridge user's payout by 12 hours.

What Stripe reports: industry stablecoin payment volume around $400 billion, doubled; Bridge's volume more than quadrupled <Evidence id="stablecoin.volume.2025" />; Visa, Nubank, Shopify and Klarna testing Tempo, whose mainnet was "launching soon" as of February 2026. What Stripe doesn't report: any absolute volume for Bridge or Tempo, or what it paid for any of it. The tension is that Stripe is building a chain while selling neutrality on every other one.

## Usage billing: own both sides of an AI company's P&L

Metronome closed in January 2026. Its metering already powered OpenAI, Anthropic and NVIDIA, and Patrick Collison's stated view is that usage-based models "will be a defining feature of the next decade" <Evidence id="metronome.acquired.2026" />. Eight months later Metronome is in Stripe's own billing documentation <Evidence id="docs.billing.models" />. Then in August 2026 Stripe agreed to buy OpenRouter, which routes token usage across more than 400 models, so that businesses can "manage both sides of profitability in the AI era" <Evidence id="openrouter.agreed.2026" />.

Read that phrase again. Metering is the revenue side. Routing is the cost side. A payments company that owns both is sitting close to its customers' margin, and its most-cited customers are the most capable engineering organisations in the world. The Revenue suite's run rate is Stripe's measure of whether this is working <Evidence id="revenue-suite.run-rate.2026" />. OpenRouter hadn't completed by my cutoff.

## Agents: be the checkout wherever the purchase happens

The Agentic Commerce Protocol, written with OpenAI; Shared Payment Tokens that let an agent pay without holding credentials; an Agentic Commerce Suite; Instant Checkout inside ChatGPT; then Copilot, then Meta's Muse agent <Evidence id="agentic.launches.2025" />. Stripe's own framing puts the industry at "levels 1 and 2" of its five-level model and says success "is contingent on universal interoperability," which is why the protocol is open.

Brands onboarding, by Stripe's account: Etsy, Urban Outfitters, Coach. Transaction volume through any of it: not disclosed. The tradeoff is explicit in the design. Shared Payment Tokens work "even [for] businesses that don't process payments with Stripe." Stripe is building rails competitors can run on, and betting that owning the wallet, 250 million enrolled consumers <Evidence id="link.users.apr-2026" />, matters more than owning the rail.

<Judgment>
Three of these bets point the same direction: get closer to the money's context, whether that's an agent deciding what to buy, a model deciding what to charge, or a stablecoin deciding how to settle. The PayPal bid was the same instinct at the largest possible scale, and it's the one Stripe pulled back from. If the running three pay off, Stripe stops being a payment processor with software attached and becomes the ledger AI businesses run on. If they don't, it has bought a lot of companies for reasons its letters describe well and its results don't yet show.
</Judgment>
