Four bets. One finished in public. Three are running, and every result on them is Stripe's own account.

## The PayPal bid, made and withdrawn

Stripe and Advent offered $60.50 a share, about $53 billion, on 14 July 2026. PayPal called it insufficient <Evidence id="paypal.bid.rejected.2026" />. By 13 August PayPal's shares had passed the bid, and on 27 August the consortium walked away <Evidence id="paypal.bid.timeline.2026" />. Axios's sourced explanation: the two sides never agreed on why the price had risen. A higher bid would have paid for the excitement the bid itself created.

I've marked it **mixed**. It cost nothing disclosed, and it revealed both an appetite nobody had seen and a price Stripe won't cross.

## Stablecoins: buy the rail

Bridge closed in February 2025 <Evidence id="bridge.acquired.2025" />, then Privy, Financial Accounts in 100+ countries, and Tempo, a payments blockchain built with Paradigm <Evidence id="tempo.2025" />. Stripe's bet is that agents will soon make most internet transactions and today's chains can't carry them. It reports stablecoin volume around $400 billion and Bridge volume more than quadrupling <Evidence id="stablecoin.volume.2025" />, with no absolute figures and no prices paid.

## Usage billing: both sides of an AI company's P&L

Metronome closed in January 2026, already metering OpenAI, Anthropic and NVIDIA <Evidence id="metronome.acquired.2026" />. In August Stripe agreed to buy OpenRouter, which routes spending across 400+ models, to help businesses "manage both sides of profitability in the AI era" <Evidence id="openrouter.agreed.2026" />. Metering is the revenue side, routing is the cost side: a payments company owning both sits close to its customers' margin.

## Agents: be the checkout wherever the purchase happens

The Agentic Commerce Protocol with OpenAI, Shared Payment Tokens, Instant Checkout in ChatGPT, then Copilot and Meta's Muse <Evidence id="agentic.launches.2025" />. The protocol is open and the tokens work for merchants who don't use Stripe. The bet is that owning the wallet, 250 million enrolled consumers <Evidence id="link.users.apr-2026" />, matters more than owning the rail.

<Judgment>
Three of these bets point the same way: get closer to the money's context, whether that's an agent deciding what to buy, a model deciding what to charge, or a stablecoin deciding how to settle. The PayPal bid was the same instinct at the largest scale, and it's the one Stripe pulled back from.
</Judgment>
