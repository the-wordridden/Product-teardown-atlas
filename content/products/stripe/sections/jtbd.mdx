Two jobs, each with a source that isn't Stripe. People write about leaving a payments provider far more than about choosing one, so this is thinner than the company's size deserves.

## Job one: sell everywhere without a bank negotiation

*When we sell software to customers in many countries, let us take payment without a merchant account in each one.*

The 2011 job, with geography added. Apify chose Braintree first because it was "the only major global provider supporting European companies at the time," added Stripe when it "caught up and expanded its capabilities," and kept both for redundancy <Evidence id="apify.migration.2025" />.

- **Push:** a provider that won't cover your country, or a bank process before your first sale.
- **Pull:** one integration, local payment methods everywhere, no fixed fees.
- **Anxiety:** holds with nobody to call <Evidence id="hn.account-holds" />, and one provider being a single point of failure.
- **Habit:** the integration you already have. Apify's move of 10,000 users and 9,000 cards took 18 working days.

## Job two: bill usage without building billing

*When our product charges by usage or seat, let us meter and invoice it without writing a billing system.*

Billing handles flat, per-seat, usage-based and tiered models, with Metronome for complex metering <Evidence id="docs.billing.models" />, at 0.7% of billed volume <Evidence id="pricing.products.2026" />. The alternatives are writing it yourself, or Chargebee, Chargify and Lago <Evidence id="hn.alternatives.2023" />.

The anxiety is in Stripe's own docs: if you leave, it "doesn't export your account's payment history, subscriptions, or other objects" <Evidence id="docs.pan-export.link-exclusion" />. Billing next to the payment rail is easy to adopt. It's the part that doesn't come out.

<Inference>
Neither job is "process my payments cheaply". Both are about not building something: a bank relationship, a billing system. That's why the headline rate can stay where it is.
</Inference>
