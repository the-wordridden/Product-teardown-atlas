## What the evidence establishes

A price unchanged for fifteen years <Evidence id="pricing.launch.2011" /> <Evidence id="pricing.us.2026" />, with the surcharges around it raised in 2023 <Evidence id="pricing.change.june-2023" />. A stack in which every product is a further slice of the same payment flow <Evidence id="pricing.products.2026" />. A wallet that enrols consumers with Stripe rather than any merchant <Evidence id="docs.link.enrolment" />, and an export policy that returns every saved card except those saved through it <Evidence id="docs.pan-export.link-exclusion" />. And a $53 billion bid, made and withdrawn within six weeks <Evidence id="paypal.bid.timeline.2026" />.

## My judgment

<Judgment>
Three sentences to keep apart. "The headline price is unchanged" is verified, from two sources fifteen years apart. "Revenue growth comes from products attached to payments" is an inference from public pricing and reported run rates <Evidence id="revenue-suite.run-rate.2026" />, and I'd defend it. "Link is Stripe's moat" is a judgment built on a mechanism Stripe documents and a consequence nobody outside Stripe has yet reported. The first two are the teardown. The third is the one I'd most want to be argued with about.

What would change my mind, in order: a merchant describing losing Link-saved customers on leaving; an audited group figure, to test the 0.4% yield; and any independent measurement of one link in the growth loop.
</Judgment>
