One mechanism clears the bar, and Stripe wrote the evidence for it.

## Easy in, hard out

Stripe's export page opens with a promise: "We believe our customers own the sensitive data they entrust to Stripe." It will hand your saved cards to any Level 1 processor. Then, on the same page: "You can't transfer the payment credentials saved with Link between payment processors," and Stripe "doesn't export your account's payment history, subscriptions, or other objects" <Evidence id="docs.pan-export.link-exclusion" />.

Put that next to how Link works. Consumers enrol with Stripe, not the merchant <Evidence id="docs.link.enrolment" />, and one customer takes 58% of its volume through Link <Evidence id="lovable.link-share.2026" />. A merchant like that could leave with 42% of its saved customers and would have to ask the rest to type their card in again. **The better Link does at a merchant's checkout, the less of that merchant's vault is portable.**

It's copyable with sustained effort: PayPal, Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay already sit in the same checkouts. The moat is exactly as wide as Link's share of a given merchant's checkouts.

The consequence is stated by Stripe, not yet observed. Apify's migration doesn't mention Link <Evidence id="apify.migration.2025" />, and the one closure account is about subscriptions, not Link <Evidence id="hn.data-refusal.2024" />.
