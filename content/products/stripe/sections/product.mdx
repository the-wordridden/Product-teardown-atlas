Stripe's product is a small set of objects that everything else attaches to. (Described from documentation: I didn't create an account.)

**A payment** is one successful charge, settled to the business's bank. **A customer** holds saved payment methods. **A subscription** charges on a schedule or by usage <Evidence id="docs.billing.models" />. **A connected account** is another business inside yours, for platforms <Evidence id="docs.connect.standard-accounts" />.

And **Link**, the object most people miss. A consumer who pays through Link is enrolled with Stripe, not with the merchant. At the next business on Stripe, Link recognises them by email, phone or cookie, sends a one-time code and fills in their card and address <Evidence id="docs.link.enrolment" />. Stripe says over 200 million people are enrolled <Evidence id="link.users.feb-2026" />.

## Connect is where the risk lives

For platforms, liability follows the charge type. On a direct charge, the seller's balance takes refunds and disputes; on destination and separate charges, the platform's does; on legacy Express and Custom accounts, the platform is "responsible for disputes and fraud" <Evidence id="docs.connect.charge-types" />. A Standard connected account is "a conventional Stripe account" with its own Dashboard <Evidence id="docs.connect.standard-accounts" />, so a Shopify store owner is a Stripe customer in their own right.

## Capital is a data product

Eligibility is computed daily from the account's own processing history, three months minimum. The money comes from partner lenders, Celtic Bank, Lead Bank, YouLend or Fundbox, and is repaid as a fixed slice of each future charge <Evidence id="docs.capital.underwriting" />. Stripe underwrites; someone else lends.

Radar screens every attempt for a per-transaction fee, in four plans <Evidence id="docs.radar.plans" />. And Stripe documents a way out: it exports saved cards to any Level 1 processor, with two exceptions covered under Moats <Evidence id="docs.pan-export.link-exclusion" />.

<Inference>
Three claims that sound alike and aren't. "Link autofills a returning customer at any Stripe merchant" is a documented capability. "So Link raises conversion" is Stripe's claim, not independently measured. "So merchants choose Stripe for Link" is causal, and nothing here shows it.
</Inference>
