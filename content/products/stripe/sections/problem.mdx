In 2011, taking a card payment online meant a merchant account from a bank, a separate gateway, and card numbers passing through your own servers. None of it had anything to do with what you were building.

TechCrunch's launch story named what Stripe removed: "you don't need a merchant account or gateway and Stripe will handle everything" <Evidence id="pricing.launch.2011" />. The price was 2.9% plus 30 cents per successful charge, with no setup or monthly fees, and card details went straight to Stripe.

The founders said who they were selling to: "enabling transactions on the web is a problem rooted in code, not finance." The buyer was the person writing the checkout, not the person signing bank forms.

<Inference>
Why 2011? My reading is that the buyer had changed. The person deciding how a web business took money was increasingly the developer building it, while incumbents still sold through banks to finance teams. Stripe sold to the coder. That fits the launch coverage, but it's one contemporaneous source, not a measurement.
</Inference>

Fifteen years later the price is the same: 2.9% + 30¢, no setup fees, no monthly fees <Evidence id="pricing.us.2026" />. The problem it was founded on is still the one it prices against. What changed is everything sold on top.
