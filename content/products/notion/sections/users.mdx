Notion's pricing page names four audiences, in order: individuals organising personal projects, small teams and professionals, growing businesses, and organisations needing scalability and control <Evidence id="pricing.gates.2026" />. That's the ladder the company sells.

The template gallery tells a different story about who actually shows up.

## The biggest group doesn't pay

Browse the gallery and count. Personal Planner has 9,530 templates. Student Life has 7,472. Study Planner has 5,463. Student Planner has 3,130 <Evidence id="gallery.categories.observed" />. The largest categories by a wide margin are personal and academic, not work.

Those counts measure what creators make, not how many users exist. But creators make what people search for, and a free plan with no limit for one person means this entire population can use Notion forever and never appear in revenue <Evidence id="pricing.plans.2026" />.

## The group the pricing is built for

Small teams. The moment a second member joins a free workspace, it drops to a 1,000-block cap <Evidence id="block.limit.free" />. Notion's own line for Plus is small teams and professionals working together, and the 2019 accounts show this segment forming in real time: teams walking away from Asana and Jira because they wanted to shape their own workflow <Evidence id="jtbd.team-tasks.2019" />.

## Enterprises

SSO, SCIM, audit logs, domain management, zero data retention with model providers, a customer success manager, custom pricing <Evidence id="pricing.gates.2026" />. This is the only tier where Notion sells control rather than flexibility. Nothing I opened describes a sales motion behind it.

## The fourth group, which is also a supply chain

Over 22,000 people have public creator profiles <Evidence id="gallery.scale.2026" />. They are users, suppliers and a distribution channel at once: they build the inventory the gallery ranks on.

What any of them earns isn't in this teardown, and the absence is deliberate. The gallery advertises that creators can get paid. Every earnings figure I could find came from a blog selling a course about selling templates, which isn't evidence, so there's nothing here. For a marketplace this size, that's a strange hole in the public record.

<Inference>
The behaviour came before the marketplace. At the January 2019 launch, a user had already built a website for sharing Notion pages so other people could see what was possible <Evidence id="sharing.predates-gallery.2019" />. Notion productised something its users invented, four years later. That's a different story from building a growth channel and recruiting people into it, and it's the more interesting one.
</Inference>

## Who it isn't for

One team lead, in 2020, having tried Notion as a wiki, a project tracker and an issue tracker: "Flexibility of blocks is a cognitive overhead for most folks in my team. They would rather prefer more constrained and opinionated approaches like Trello", and Notion is "a jack of all trades and master of none" <Evidence id="flexibility.cost.2020" />. That's the clearest statement anyone has made of what this product trades away, and it came from someone who wanted it to work.

And anyone who needs to leave easily. More on that in the moats section, because it turns out to be the most durable thing here.
