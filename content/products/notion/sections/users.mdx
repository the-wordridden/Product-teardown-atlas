Notion's pricing page names its audiences in order: individuals, small teams, growing businesses, organisations <Evidence id="pricing.gates.2026" />. The template gallery tells you who actually shows up.

**The biggest group doesn't pay.** The largest template categories are Personal Planner (9,530), Student Life (7,472) and Study Planner (5,463) <Evidence id="gallery.categories.observed" />. Those count what creators make, not users, but creators make what people search for. One person is free forever <Evidence id="pricing.plans.2026" />, so this population never shows up in revenue.

**The group the pricing is built for** is small teams. A second member caps a free workspace at 1,000 blocks <Evidence id="block.limit.free" />, and the 2019 accounts show that segment forming as teams left Asana and Jira <Evidence id="jtbd.team-tasks.2019" />.

**Enterprises** buy SSO, SCIM, audit logs and zero data retention with model providers <Evidence id="pricing.gates.2026" />: the only tier sold on control rather than flexibility.

**Creators** are the fourth group and a supply chain: over 22,000 public profiles build the inventory the gallery ranks on <Evidence id="gallery.scale.2026" />. What any of them earns isn't here; every figure I found came from blogs selling courses about selling templates.

The behaviour came before the marketplace. In January 2019 a user had already built a site for sharing Notion pages <Evidence id="sharing.predates-gallery.2019" />. Notion productised something its users invented, four years later.
