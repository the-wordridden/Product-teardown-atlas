Two jobs, both quoted from the same place: the January 2019 Hacker News thread where Notion was first shown to a large technical audience. That's one dated corpus of one kind of reader, and I'd rather say so than pretend it's a census.

## Job one: run our work without adopting Jira's process

*When my team needs to track work, give us something we can shape ourselves so we don't have to take on someone else's process.*

The push is in the quote: a team "switched to it after getting annoyed with Asana and not wanting to use something like Jira" <Evidence id="jtbd.team-tasks.2019" />. Another team used it "in lieu of JIRA."

The pull is in the same sentence, and it's the important half: "We love how freeform it is and allowed us to create a workflow that fit us." Not features. Fit.

The anxiety is the mirror image, and it's real. A team lead who tried Notion as a wiki, tracker and issue tracker called the flexibility "a cognitive overhead" and said his team would rather have something constrained like Trello <Evidence id="flexibility.cost.2020" />. The thing that wins this job is the thing that loses it.

The habit is an existing tracker with history in it, and colleagues who know where things live.

## Job two: find my own notes again

*When I keep notes for myself, let me structure them so I can find things again without guessing the right keyword.*

Notice what this job isn't about. Nobody in the thread said Notion was a better place to write. They said it was a better place to look things up later. One user described drafting emails into an unorganised OneNote notebook and then having to search and hope he remembered the right words <Evidence id="jtbd.personal-wiki.2019" />. Another said Notion removed the distinction between files and folders, which made it easier to structure than Google Docs.

The pull is nesting and linking: structure that emerges as you work instead of filing you have to remember to do.

The anxiety cuts both ways. Getting years of Evernote notes in, which one user said he couldn't imagine doing. And getting anything out, which another user named at launch: "I feel it's hard to get stuff out of it" <Evidence id="export.friction.2019" />.

<Inference>
Both jobs are about arrangement rather than capability. Neither person wanted a better editor or a better tracker; they wanted to stop maintaining the gap between the two. That fits a product whose only primitive is a block, and it explains why the same flexibility shows up as the main reason people love it and the main reason people bounce off it.
</Inference>

There's no third job here for AI, even though AI is now more than half of something at this company. No first-hand account of anyone hiring Notion for its AI exists in what I opened, only Notion's own product claims, so I haven't written one.
