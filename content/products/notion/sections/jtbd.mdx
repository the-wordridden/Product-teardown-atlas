Two jobs, both quoted from the January 2019 launch thread: one dated corpus of one kind of reader, not a census.

## Job one: run our work without adopting Jira's process

*When my team needs to track work, give us something we can shape ourselves.*

A team "switched to it after getting annoyed with Asana and not wanting to use something like Jira", and the pull is the same sentence: "We love how freeform it is and allowed us to create a workflow that fit us" <Evidence id="jtbd.team-tasks.2019" />. Fit, not features.

The anxiety is the mirror image: a lead who found the flexibility "a cognitive overhead" and went back to Trello <Evidence id="flexibility.cost.2020" />. What wins this job is what loses it.

## Job two: find my own notes again

*When I keep notes for myself, let me structure them so I can find things without guessing the keyword.*

Nobody said Notion was a better place to write. They said it was a better place to look things up: one user used to draft emails into an unorganised OneNote notebook and search and hope <Evidence id="jtbd.personal-wiki.2019" />. The anxiety runs both ways: porting years of notes in, and getting anything out. "I feel it's hard to get stuff out of it" <Evidence id="export.friction.2019" />.

<Inference>
Both jobs are about arrangement, not capability. That fits a product whose only primitive is a block, and explains why flexibility is both the main reason people love it and the main reason they bounce.
</Inference>
