One mechanism clears the bar, and it's the oldest complaint about the product: **getting out is hard.**

A workspace is nested blocks, databases, relations and links, all the same addressable object <Evidence id="block.api.primitive" />. No format outside Notion holds that shape. Export gives you files, not the structure that made them useful.

Users said so at launch: "I feel it's hard to get stuff out of it" <Evidence id="export.friction.2019" />. In 2021 a customer since 2017 requested an export, never got the link, and three months later got the same backlog reply. It "locks us with them" <Evidence id="export.failure.2021" />. Another user never started because "I don't fancy the idea of moving everything" <Evidence id="lockin.anxiety.2020" />. Some people price in the lock-in before they arrive.

**Durability: copyable with sustained effort.** Open-source and local-first alternatives exist; none has been shown to move a real workspace with databases and relations intact. The moat is a fidelity gap, and fidelity gaps close when someone funds the work.

**How to attack it:** faithful import of Notion workspaces, or a local-first tool that makes the question irrelevant.

## What I won't count

- **The gallery isn't a network effect.** It has the shape of one, but nothing shows an existing user's value rising when a specific new user joins. Thirty thousand templates is inventory.
- **Scale isn't a mechanism.** Millions of CRDT operations a minute <Evidence id="crdt.architecture.2026" /> says the system is big, not why a rival can't be built.
- **Flexibility isn't defensibility.** At least one lead found it the reason to leave <Evidence id="flexibility.cost.2020" />.

## The counter-evidence

Notion bought Skiff in February 2024 and shut it down within twelve months without converting a single account <Evidence id="acquisition.skiff.2024" />. Users who worry about depending on a company that might switch things off have watched this one do it.
