One mechanism clears the bar, and it's the oldest complaint about this product.

## Getting out

A Notion workspace is a nested structure of blocks, databases, relations and internal links. The API confirms that isn't a metaphor: pages are blocks, blocks nest, everything is the same addressable object <Evidence id="block.api.primitive" />. No format outside Notion holds that shape. Exporting gives you files; it doesn't give you the structure that made the workspace useful.

Users named the consequence immediately. At the January 2019 launch: "I feel it's hard to get stuff out of it" <Evidence id="export.friction.2019" />.

Two years later, a customer since 2017 described the export feature simply not working. He requested his data, the download link never arrived, and three months later support gave him the same reply about a large backlog with no immediate fix. His framing is the useful part: a critical function that "locks us with them", against a product sold on the promise that you own your data <Evidence id="export.failure.2021" />.

Two independent accounts, two years apart, on the same friction. And a third user, in 2020, explaining why he never started: if Notion ever shuts down or gets outshone, "I don't fancy the idea of moving everything" <Evidence id="lockin.anxiety.2020" />. The lock-in is priced in by some people before they arrive.

**Durability: copyable with sustained effort.** Several open-source and local-first alternatives exist and are well regarded. None has been shown to move a real workspace across cleanly, with databases and relations intact. The moat isn't the idea; it's the fidelity gap, and fidelity gaps close when someone funds the work.

**How to attack it:** a competitor that imports Notion workspaces faithfully, or a local-first tool that makes the question irrelevant.

## What I won't count

**The gallery isn't a network effect.** It has the shape of one: more creators, more templates, more intents covered. But the formal test asks whether an existing user's value rises when a *specific* new user joins, and nothing here shows that. Thirty thousand templates is inventory, and inventory is not the same as a network. I'm not classifying one in either direction.

**Scale isn't a mechanism.** Millions of CRDT operations a minute <Evidence id="crdt.architecture.2026" /> tells you the system is big. It doesn't tell you why a rival can't be built.

**Flexibility isn't defensibility.** It's the product's core trade, and at least one team lead experienced it as the reason to leave rather than stay <Evidence id="flexibility.cost.2020" />.

## The counter-evidence

Notion's own acquisition record cuts against its lock-in story. It bought Skiff, an encrypted docs and mail suite, in February 2024 and shut it down within twelve months without converting a single account <Evidence id="acquisition.skiff.2024" />. Users who worry aloud about depending on a company that might switch things off have watched that company switch something off.

<EvidenceGap>
**What holds up:** a documented structural reason leaving is hard, and three independent users describing the friction across six years.

**What doesn't:** the cost. Nobody has published what a failed or expensive migration away from Notion actually cost a business. The friction is corroborated. The consequence is still an anecdote.
</EvidenceGap>
