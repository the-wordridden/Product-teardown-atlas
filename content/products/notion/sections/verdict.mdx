## What the evidence establishes

A public catalogue of 30,000 templates in 348 categories, each one a landing page for a specific intent <Evidence id="gallery.scale.2026" />. Search traffic to that catalogue estimated at 287,000 visits a month across 60,000 US keywords <Evidence id="gallery.traffic.ahrefs.2023" />. Pages that render the entire working artefact to a stranger with no account <Evidence id="gallery.artefact.observed" />, and a documented wall at the moment they want to keep it: "you'll be prompted to sign in or create an account first" <Evidence id="gallery.signup-gate" />. Fifty-one million duplications from nearly eleven million people in one year <Evidence id="gallery.duplications.2023" />.

And a business model that starts free and unlimited for one person, then caps the workspace at 1,000 lifetime blocks the moment a second person joins <Evidence id="block.limit.free" />.

## What the evidence suggests

<Inference>
These are one design, not two. Free-forever-for-one manufactures the people who make the templates; the templates rank; the rankings bring strangers; the wall converts them; some of them become creators. Notion removed the individual block cap in 2020 <Evidence id="block.limit.history.2020" /> and the gallery grew fiftyfold. That sequence is consistent across every source I opened, though no one has demonstrated the causal link, and Notion's own relaunch post frames the gallery as a showcase for creators rather than a growth channel <Evidence id="gallery.scale.2023" />.
</Inference>

## What stays open

How many of those eleven million people were new. Whether anyone who duplicates a template comes back. What a template creator earns. What any of the five acquisitions cost. How many people use Notion at all, a number the company declines to publish even on its own About page <Evidence id="notion.no-numbers" />. And whether the AI claim means what it sounds like.

## My judgment

<Judgment>
The central call, mine and not a fact: **Notion gives one person everything free and charges when a second person joins, and its template gallery manufactures those first people at search scale.** Free-forever-for-one supplies the creators, the creators supply the gallery, the gallery supplies the individuals, and the second member is where the money is. One design, not four.

What the evidence won't let me say is the part everyone else says. The gallery is the best documented conversion surface I've examined and the least documented acquisition channel: 51 million duplications published, and never once how many were new.

Three sentences that need to stay apart. "A logged-out visitor must create an account to take a template" is verified, from Notion's own documentation and my own observation. "The gallery converts at scale" is a sound inference from that mechanism plus Notion's duplication figure. "The gallery acquires users" is the claim everyone makes about this company, including the brief I started from, and it is the one thing the evidence does not establish.

That gap isn't an accident of my research. I looked for four passes. Notion will tell you its editor processes millions of CRDT operations a minute <Evidence id="crdt.architecture.2026" />. It will tell you duplications and unique duplicators. The new-versus-existing split lives in the same database as the number it did publish.

What would change my mind fastest: any statement from Notion separating new signups from existing users at the duplication step. Second, a creator describing where their template traffic comes from. Third, an audited figure of any kind, which would also settle whether "more than half of ARR from AI-enabled customers" <Evidence id="ai.arr-share.2026" /> means what a reader would assume it means.

One note on method. This is the first teardown in the Atlas where I could watch the mechanism myself rather than read about it, and it shows: the strongest evidence here is a button I clicked. It's also the teardown where the company's own account of its problem is thinner than its users'. Both of those are unusual, and both made the work better.
</Judgment>

## Open questions

The five that shape this verdict most: the new-versus-existing split at duplication, retention after a template lands, what creators earn, what the acquisitions cost, and what "AI-enabled customers" actually measures.
