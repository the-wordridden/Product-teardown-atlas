Start with what Notion doesn't say. Its own About page has a mission, a quotation from Alan Kay, and not a single number: no users, no customers, no headcount, no funding total <Evidence id="notion.no-numbers" />. For a company at an eleven billion dollar valuation, that's a choice.

Here's what is on the record. In January 2026 Sequoia, Index and GIC bought about $270 million of shares from current and former employees at an $11 billion valuation <Evidence id="valuation.tender.2026" />. That's secondary: money went to employees, not into the company. Six weeks earlier Forbes reported the round at about $300 million and added the numbers Notion hasn't published itself, attributed to people familiar with the matter: ARR recently past $600 million, and the company cash flow positive <Evidence id="arr.2025.reported" />.

Put the valuation marks in a row and something stands out. A $275 million Series C at $10 billion in October 2021, up from $2 billion eighteen months earlier <Evidence id="funding.series-c.2021" />. A tender at $10 billion in 2022. A tender at $11 billion in 2026 <Evidence id="valuation.history" />. Five years, ten percent.

## The one number Notion does publish

It's in an engineering post, not a press release. Describing how concurrent editing works, Notion says the system went live in July 2025 and is "one of the largest CRDT deployments in the world, as we process millions of CRDT operations every minute" <Evidence id="crdt.architecture.2026" />.

That's the only first-party scale figure I found anywhere. The company will tell you how many merge operations its editor handles per minute and won't tell you how many people use it.

## What I can't tell you

Headcount. There's no primary source, and I've left it unestablished rather than repeat the figure that circulates.

Founding year is the one field on this page not sourced to the same standard as the rest. I've used 2013, which is what secondary sources agree on, but no document I opened states it, and I'd rather flag that than let it pass as though it were verified like everything else here.

<Judgment>
The shape of the disclosure tells you something. Notion publishes engineering internals in detail and business facts almost not at all. Every user number you've seen quoted for this company traces back to an aggregator, not to Notion. When a private company is this specific about its database and this quiet about its customers, the quiet part is the part worth noticing.
</Judgment>
