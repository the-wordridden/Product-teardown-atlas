Four plans: Free, Plus at $10, Business at $20, Enterprise on request, all per member per month <Evidence id="pricing.plans.2026" />. That's the ordinary part. The interesting part is the free tier, which behaves unlike almost any other.

## The cap that counts what you made, not what you keep

A Free workspace with one person has no block limit. Add a second member and the whole workspace drops to 1,000 blocks <Evidence id="block.limit.free" />.

Read Notion's own description of how that counts: "Every piece of content you add is counted as a new block", and "Deleting blocks, or emptying the trash, will not reduce your block count." It's a lifetime-created meter, not a storage meter. You cannot tidy your way back under it. You get a three-day grace period, after which you can still edit everything you have and can't add anything new.

So the monetisation moment isn't volume. It's **inviting the second person**. Collaboration is the trigger.

## This used to apply to everyone

In May 2020 Notion removed the cap for individuals and folded the old $4 a month Personal plan into a free tier. A user in that discussion quoted the FAQ of the day: the free plan's block count "is not limited anymore", and existing Personal subscribers were "automatically upgraded to our new Personal Pro Plan at no extra cost" <Evidence id="block.limit.history.2020" />.

Before that, individuals hit the same wall, with the same cruelty. One of them: "hit some arbitrary 1000 block limit that no matter what I deleted I couldn't get rid of."

<Inference>
Look at what that change did. Notion gave up a $4 a month revenue line and in exchange got an unbounded population of individuals who could build anything they liked for free. Those are the people who now fill a gallery of 30,000 templates. The 2020 decision and the growth engine described two sections down are the same decision.
</Inference>

## AI is the tier, not an add-on

Notion AI is "available on Business and Enterprise Plans"; Free and Plus get "a limited number of complimentary AI responses" to try it <Evidence id="ai.plan-gate.2026" />. Premium models spend credits and need an admin to switch them on.

So AI isn't a product you buy. It's the argument for paying $20 instead of $10, across every seat.

Which makes the revenue claim interesting. Notion says "more than 50% of our ARR came from AI-enabled customers, and that percentage more than doubled over the last year" <Evidence id="ai.arr-share.2026" />. Forbes, reporting the same period, said half of ARR "is coming from its artificial intelligence products" <Evidence id="ai.revenue-share.forbes" />.

Those are not the same sentence. One counts revenue from customers who use AI, which includes every dollar of a Business subscription bought for any reason. The other counts revenue earned by AI. Nothing reconciles them, and I'm not going to merge them, because the difference is the whole question.

## The shape of it

Reported ARR recently past $600 million, cash flow positive, on figures nobody has audited <Evidence id="arr.2025.reported" />. No margin, no mix, no customer count.

<Judgment>
My read: the free tier is a manufacturing cost, not a marketing cost. Notion pays to store and serve an unlimited population of individuals, and what it gets back is inventory, thirty thousand templates that rank in search and pull the next person in. The cap then sits exactly where value starts to concentrate, at the second person. It's an elegant design. It also means the company's largest user population is, by construction, the one it has chosen never to charge.
</Judgment>
