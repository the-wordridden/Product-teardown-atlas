Free, Plus at $10, Business at $20, Enterprise on request, per member per month <Evidence id="pricing.plans.2026" />. That's the ordinary part. The free tier is not.

## The cap counts what you made, not what you keep

One person on Free has no block limit. Add a second member and the workspace drops to 1,000 blocks. "Deleting blocks, or emptying the trash, will not reduce your block count" <Evidence id="block.limit.free" />. It's a lifetime meter. You can't tidy your way back under it.

So the monetisation moment is **inviting the second person**.

## It used to apply to everyone

In May 2020 Notion removed the cap for individuals and folded its $4 Personal plan into free <Evidence id="block.limit.history.2020" />. Before that, individuals hit the same wall: "no matter what I deleted I couldn't get rid of" it.

<Inference>
Notion gave up a $4 revenue line and got an unbounded population of individuals building for free. Those people fill a gallery of 30,000 templates. The 2020 pricing change and the growth engine are the same decision.
</Inference>

## AI is the tier, not an add-on

Notion AI is on Business and Enterprise; Free and Plus get a limited taste <Evidence id="ai.plan-gate.2026" />. It's the argument for $20 over $10, on every seat.

Notion says "more than 50% of our ARR came from AI-enabled customers" <Evidence id="ai.arr-share.2026" />. Forbes said half of ARR "is coming from its artificial intelligence products" <Evidence id="ai.revenue-share.forbes" />. One counts every dollar from customers who use AI. The other counts dollars earned by AI. I won't merge them; the difference is the whole question.

Reported ARR is past $600 million, cash flow positive, unaudited <Evidence id="arr.2025.reported" />.

<Judgment>
The free tier is a manufacturing cost, not a marketing cost. Notion pays to serve unlimited individuals and gets back inventory that ranks and pulls in the next person. The cap sits where value concentrates, at the second person. Elegant, and it means the largest user population is the one Notion has chosen never to charge.
</Judgment>
