The best description of the problem came from a user, not from Notion. At the January 2019 launch, one commenter called it "the perfect balance between strict JIRA/PM tools and completely unstructured wikis/text files" <Evidence id="positioning.between-tools.2019" />.

That names a real gap. On one side, trackers that make you adopt someone else's process. On the other, documents that hold anything and track nothing. Teams ran both and maintained the seam by hand.

The same thread shows what people were leaving. A team that "switched to it after getting annoyed with Asana and not wanting to use something like Jira" <Evidence id="jtbd.team-tasks.2019" />. A user for whom "Notion replaced Evernote", another who "used to draft emails in an unorganized OneNote notebook" <Evidence id="jtbd.personal-wiki.2019" />. Six tools in one thread, replaced by one.

**The wedge was a single primitive.** A block is any piece of content, blocks nest, and a page is itself a block <Evidence id="block.api.primitive" />. If a paragraph, a task and a table row are the same kind of object, a document and a database stop being different products.

<Inference>
Why 2019 rather than 2015? My reading is that the gap had widened as teams accumulated one tool per job, and someone finally sold the seam instead of another tool. Notion's own framing is a mission about early computing pioneers, not a dated problem statement.
</Inference>
